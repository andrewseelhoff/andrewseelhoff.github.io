This book shows how much of a nerd I am. In spite of that, I think it's a really interesting view of the origin story of modern thermodynamics. There's a lot of nuance in the chronology of how these things were discovered that provides unique insights to the laws of thermodynamics and other findings. Dare I say this provides the clarity that was missing in my undergraduate coursework. Too bad I'll likely stay far away from the land of thermo!

This is a short read, but it's VERY dense. It's certainly a book for mechanical engineers and is not very approachable without some broad knowledge of thermodynamics. Be ready for some very archaic texts from some of Thermo's legends such as Lord Kelvin, Carnot, Joule, and Clausius... even the author admits the primary sources that are included can be incredibly confusing to read at points. All of this to say, if you really wanna learn about thermo, pick this up!

Favorite quote (which I believe may be one of the earliest uses of the world "field" as meaning "area of research"): "How far can we advance in any of the paths which science has opened to us before we find ourselves enveloped in those thick mists which on every side bound the horizon of the human intellect? But how ample and how interesting is the field that is given us to explore!" - Count Rumford, 1798
